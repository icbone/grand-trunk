## Toronto open before 2004

By 2001, Toronto's open scene was spread across a handful of teams: Redi Knights, Y.E.S., Nerve, Love Gravy, Too Bad and Dead Baby Seals, with Wax playing out of Ottawa.

In 2002 things consolidated. Redi Knights, Y.E.S., Nerve and Ottawa's Wax combined into **GOAT**, a joint Toronto–Ottawa team. Love Gravy carried on as **Joystik**, and a new team, **Pong**, appeared. Too Bad kept going as it always had.

## Before Grand Trunk: Joystik

GOAT was Toronto's top open team, but as a combined Toronto–Ottawa roster it couldn't field a full side for local practices. Joystik filled in as Toronto's "second" open team and trained alongside GOAT.

## The breakaway

In 2004, a group of younger players decided they wanted more intensity and more commitment than they were getting. They came from three teams:

- the younger players on **Joystik**, including [Kirk](/grand-trunk/players/kirk-nylen), [Inian](/grand-trunk/players/inian-moorthy), [Shooter](/grand-trunk/players/scott-hastie), [Chua](/grand-trunk/players/shawn-chua) and [Dean](/grand-trunk/players/dean-ruhnke);
- one or two younger players from **Black Sheep**, a mixed team that later became Bombing Madd Fatties and then Union;
- some of the best up-and-coming young players from **Pong**.

They left to start Grand Trunk. **[Colin Hanes](/grand-trunk/players/colin-hanes)**, who came over from Black Sheep and the Fatties, came up with the name.

The same year, Pong disbanded after two seasons (2002–2003). Its players split two ways: a core of them formed **Roy**, and others went to Grand Trunk. Toronto's club history traces both Roy and Grand Trunk back to Pong.

## The first season

Grand Trunk practiced Tuesdays and Thursdays. On Thursdays GOAT practiced at the same field, and the two teams often scrimmaged together at the end of the night.

The inaugural season was a strong one. Grand Trunk beat Joystik and became the top all-Toronto team. Along the way they picked up the odd extra player for tournaments like No Surf in Cleveland and Motown Throwdown in Detroit.

## Birth of the club system

Grand Trunk caught GOAT's attention. Within a year or two, GOAT became an almost entirely Toronto team and brought up Grand Trunk's top players, Kirk and Inian among them. In 2005, Grand Trunk and Roy were folded into a club system alongside GOAT, with combined tryouts and top players moving up through the teams from year to year.

That's been the shape of Toronto open ever since: **GOAT, Grand Trunk and Roy** sharing resources, with Too Bad offering a home for players who don't fit the club system.

Joystik played one more season, in 2005, then folded. Its players later regrouped with former GOAT players as **Tombstone**, competing in the masters and grand masters divisions. The irony: once the under-30s left to form Grand Trunk, everyone still on Joystik was already masters-eligible.
